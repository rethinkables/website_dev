The problem

The distance between what is reported and what is real.

In any large administration, information flows upward — and thins as it climbs. A figure entered in a village register becomes a block total, becomes a district dashboard, becomes a number on a screen in the capital. At each step, the ability to check it against reality falls away. The number arrives. The ground truth does not.

This is not a failure of intent. It is a failure of instrument. Officers are asked to act on data they have no practical means to verify — to sanction meals for children they cannot confirm were present, to certify works they cannot revisit, to trust a presence no one witnessed. The gap is not dishonesty. It is the absence of a tool.

We build the instruments that close that distance — and we leave the judgment where it belongs.

How it works

Built offline-first, for the field.

  1. 01

    Capture

    Field staff record attendance or verification on an ordinary smartphone. No internet is required at the point of capture.

  2. 02

    Seal

    Each record is fingerprinted on the device the moment it is taken. It cannot be edited or backdated afterward.

  3. 03

    Sync

    When a network returns, records upload on their own to the department's dashboard — nothing is lost, nothing is delayed.

  4. 04

    See

    The officer receives a clear view of what happened on the ground, and of exactly where the record and reality diverge.

How we think about this

Why we build the way we do.

We design for adoption, not ideals.

Software fails in government not because the logic is wrong, but because it doesn't fit how people actually work. We build around field realities: connectivity, multitasking, resistance, fatigue. If officers don't adopt it, the design failed — not them.

We report arithmetic, not verdicts.

Claimed 38 meals. Verified 35. Difference of 3. That's what you see. What you do with it is yours alone. We measure; we do not accuse. The interpretation belongs to the officer.

We stay silent about what we don't know.

We won't claim field experience we don't have. We won't promise metrics we haven't tested. We won't guess at your constraints. We learn by listening, by piloting, by failing small before asking you to scale.

Risk is ours until it works.

You don't pay upfront. You don't commit capital. You don't take the reputational hit if we misunderstood your problem. We carry that. You pay only when your officers actually use it.

Wherever officers are asked to work without sight of the ground — nutrition, rural works, health, distribution — the same principle holds: you define the problem, we build the instrument, and the authority stays with you.

How we work

We assist. We do not adjudicate.

The instrument reports what the record shows. The interpretation, and the decision, belong to the officer. We are the lens, never the judge.

We report the arithmetic, not a verdict.

A claim of thirty-eight, a verified count of thirty-five, a difference of three. We present what is measured and let it speak. Drawing the conclusion is your prerogative.

We hold ourselves to the same standard.

If a pilot fails to meet the benchmark agreed before it began, we will be the first to say so. An instrument that flatters its maker is of use to no one.
